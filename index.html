Our commitment

Most IT contracts protect the provider

Ours are built the other way round. If we underperform, it costs us — not you. Service credits and on-site hot spares are included as standard on every managed service agreement we write — not premium tiers, not optional add-ons.

Standard on every managed service agreement

SLAs backed by service credits

Response and resolution targets with money attached. Miss them and you're credited automatically — no need to notice, chase or argue. An SLA without a penalty is just a promise.

Hot spare devices on site

24/7 follow-the-sun desk

Fixed monthly pricing

Our approach

Five stages, one accountable team

The same method on every engagement — so you always know which stage you're in, and what happens next.

01 Recognize Understand the business before touching the technology

Evaluate

A full audit of what you actually have — estate, licensing, contracts, security posture, and the recurring issues your team stopped reporting because nothing ever changed.

Understand

We learn how the business makes money and where IT sits in that. Which systems are critical at month-end, which outages cost you customers rather than just patience.

Engage

We talk to the people using the systems, not only the people buying them. Adoption problems almost always start with a decision made without them.

02 Consult Agree what good looks like, in your terms rather than ours

Advise

We tell you what we would fix first and why, including the fixes that cost nothing. Where you don't need a service we sell, we say so.

Collaborate

The plan is built with your team rather than presented to them. The people who have to live with the decision get a say in making it.

Aim

Measurable targets agreed upfront — uptime, response, cost per user. Success is defined before we begin, not argued over afterwards.

03 Adapt Design the change around how your people actually work

Align

The design is mapped to your operating rhythm, compliance obligations and budget cycle — not to whatever we happen to be best at deploying.

Design

We architect for resilience and for the skill level of the people running it day to day. An elegant system nobody can operate is a future outage.

Prepare

Everything is staged, tested and documented before it touches production, with the rollback path agreed in advance rather than improvised.

04 Deliver Make the change happen, and prove it landed

Transform

Migration and modernisation in planned phases, with the business running throughout. No weekend heroics, no downtime nobody was warned about.

Implement

Deployment comes with communications, training and floor-walking, so adoption happens in the first week rather than eventually.

Prove

We hand over against the targets set at stage two and show the evidence — including anything we missed and what we did about it.

05 Manage Keep improving it, not merely keeping it alive

Proactive

Round-the-clock monitoring and patching that closes issues before your team notices them. Most of our work should be invisible to you.

Maintain

Backups tested, licensing reviewed, hardware refreshed on schedule, and documentation kept current as the estate changes.

Improve

Scheduled service reviews with honest reporting, and a roadmap that actually moves — so year three looks nothing like year one.

Talk to an engineer

Thirty minutes, no charge, no obligation

How much does managed IT actually cost?
Most clients land around $250 per user per month, depending on the level of security and support you need. It's a fixed monthly figure agreed upfront — no hourly billing, and no extra invoice when something breaks. We'll give you a firm number after the consultation, not a range.
Are we locked into a long contract?
We offer 12-month, 3-year and 5-year terms, and the longer ones carry real discounts — up to a full year free on a 5-year agreement. Length never means trapped, though: on any term, if we miss your service levels for three consecutive months you can give three months' notice and end the agreement — and we then transition you to your new provider free of charge. We'd rather earn year four than litigate it.
We already have an IT provider. How hard is switching?
Easier than most people expect. We handle the handover, document what your current provider holds, and run both setups in parallel until we're confident nothing is missing. Typical transition is 2–4 weeks with no downtime, and we do the awkward parts — chasing admin credentials, domain access, license transfers — so you don't have to.
What happens to our in-house IT person?
They usually get their job back. We take the monitoring, patching, tickets and out-of-hours cover off their plate so they can focus on the projects that actually need someone who knows your business. We work alongside internal teams as often as we replace them.
How quickly do you respond when something breaks?
Critical issues — anything stopping people working — are picked up in under 15 minutes on average, with response times written into your agreement rather than left vague. Around 93% of tickets are resolved by the first engineer who touches them, so you're not repeating yourself.
Are we too small for you?
Probably not. We work with businesses from around 10 users upwards. Below that, honestly, you may not need a full managed service yet — and if that's the case we'll tell you on the call rather than sell you something you don't need.
Where is your team based?
Management sits in the UK, operations run from the US, and service delivery is global. That structure is why we can genuinely staff 24/7 — an incident at 3am reaches an engineer who's mid-shift rather than one dragged out of bed, which is the difference between a fix and a holding response. Your account contact stays the same person regardless of who picks up the ticket.
What actually happens on the consultation?
Thirty minutes with an engineer, not a salesperson. You talk, we listen, and we tell you what we'd do about it. By the end you'll know what it would cost and what we'd commit to in writing — and if we're not the right answer for you, we'll say so on the call rather than three emails later.
